The Science of Structural Drying in Spartanburg, SC

Home / Blog / The Science of Structural Drying in Spartanburg, SC

Few Spartanburg, SC homeowners know much about structural drying, even though the rest of a restoration job depends on it. Once the visible water is gone, a lot is still held in the building itself, in framing lumber, drywall, insulation, subflooring, and concrete. Mold, lingering smells, and deteriorating materials are the result when the trapped water is not taken out correctly. This article explains the science behind professional structural drying and why it produces far better results than DIY attempts.

What Psychrometry Means for Drying

Professional structural drying is based on psychrometry, the study of the physical and thermodynamic properties of gas-vapor mixtures. Put simply, it is about the way air movement, relative humidity, and temperature work together to pull water from wet materials. IICRC-certified technicians use psychrometric calculations to determine the equipment setup each drying situation needs, including the type and number of dehumidifiers, the positioning of air movers, and the target temperature and humidity levels.

The Equipment Professionals Use

The equipment used for professional structural drying operates on an entirely different scale than consumer products. LGR (Low-Grain Refrigerant) dehumidifiers can remove 15 to 30 or more pints of moisture per hour from the air, compared to 2 to 3 pints per hour for a residential unit. Evaporation is sped up by air movers that deliver a directed, high-velocity stream of 2,500 CFM or more across wet surfaces. Under extreme moisture loads or in cold weather, the specialized tool is a desiccant dehumidifier. The dollars invested in a full set run to tens of thousands, and it only performs properly in trained hands.

The Drying Process Step by Step

There is a defined method behind every structural drying project. It begins with thermal imaging and moisture meters, which are used to map every area the water reached. This map guides equipment placement and establishes baseline moisture readings. Dehumidifiers and air movers are positioned according to psychrometric calculations to create the best conditions for evaporation and moisture removal. Adding heat is an option when a quicker evaporation rate is needed. Each day a technician comes back, reads every monitoring point, moves or changes equipment if required, and writes down the progress. Drying continues until all affected materials reach acceptable moisture levels compared to dry reference standards.

How Many Days Will Drying Take?

Expect 3 to 5 days for the majority of residential jobs when proper commercial gear is used. How long it takes comes down to how much moisture there is, which materials got wet (drywall dries far faster than concrete), how the building is constructed, the ambient conditions, and how many machines of what type are running. As a rule, standard drywall needs 2 to 3 days, wood framing 3 to 4 days, and concrete 5 to 7 days or more. You will get a projected finish date from the initial readings, and it is revised as the structure dries.

The Reason We Monitor Every Day

The difference between a professional job and guessing is monitoring every day. Only calibrated readings can tell you if a material is drying at all, if it is on pace, and if it has reached a safe moisture level. Deep in a material, moisture can remain at a dangerous level even though the surface feels dry when touched. Verifying it takes a moisture meter that has been calibrated. Professional drying documentation also gives your insurance company proof that proper industry protocols were followed, supporting your claim and protecting your property value.
