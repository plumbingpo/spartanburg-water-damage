Wall and Drywall Water Damage: What Spartanburg Homeowners Should Know

Think of drywall as a wick. Once the base of a wall gets wet, moisture climbs through the paper and gypsum, commonly a foot or more higher than the water stood on the floor. From the room, the surface may seem dry and sound even though the hidden face and the insulation are saturated. That is why we check walls with a moisture meter instead of trusting how they look.

A wet wall does not automatically mean demolition. When the water was clean and the drywall remains firm, drying it where it stands is often possible, at times by pulling the baseboard and pushing air into the cavity through small holes. When the wall is soft, crumbling, moldy or was soaked by dirty water, we make a flood cut, a straight line above the damage, and remove everything below it.

Signs to Look For

  • Soft or crumbly spots you notice when pushing lightly on the wall
  • Flooring along the wall that is damp, stained or starting to lift
  • Bubbling, wrinkled or peeling paint and wallpaper low on the wall
  • Dark spots or fuzzy growth at corners and behind furniture
  • Nail or screw heads popping out and seams showing through the paint
  • Caulk lines cracking or gaps opening where trim meets the wall
Behind the Surface

The Damage You Do Not See

Mold Inside Cavities

Mold thrives on the rear face of drywall because it is paper covered, dark and has no moving air. It can cover the hidden side while the painted face looks clean.

Water Next Door

Water passes under the bottom plate into the next room or closet. Every wall involved is tested on its two faces, and so is the adjacent flooring.

Wet Outlets and Wiring

Receptacles and cables sit low in the wall, right where water collects. Do not use outlets on a wet wall until the circuit has been checked.

Moisture in Exterior Walls

On an exterior wall, water can soak the sheathing behind the insulation. Because it is slow to dry, we test it before any new drywall goes up.

How We Fix It

Our Wall and Drywall Water Damage Process

1

Find Every Wet Area

Using meter readings and thermal images, our team outlines where the water went, upward and sideways, on both faces of the wall and in neighboring rooms.

2

Decide What Stays

Based on the water category, how long it sat and how the drywall feels, we tell you plainly which sections can be dried in place and which need to come out.

3

Open Up or Air Out

Where the drywall stays, the trim is pulled and airflow is sent inside the wall. For walls being removed, we make a level flood cut and take out wet insulation.

4

Dry and Rebuild

Air movers and dehumidifiers run until studs and remaining drywall read dry, typically 3 to 5 days. After that comes insulation, new drywall hung and finished, trim put back and paint.

The Clock

How the Damage Progresses

First hours

Wicking Begins

Moisture enters where the panel meets the floor and wicks upward from there. Baseboards and the bottom plate take on moisture.

24 to 48 hours

Swelling and Blisters

Moisture keeps climbing, the baseboards puff up and blisters form in the paint. Inside the cavity, the paper face becomes a place mold can begin.

Days 3 through 7

Panels Lose Strength

Paper peels from a core that is no longer firm. A musty odor grows stronger while the batts settle and mold covers more of the unseen face.

Past one week

Damage Reaches the Studs

Studs and plates stay wet long enough to grow mold and begin to decay. A far larger section of wall needs tearing out and rebuilding.
