Bathroom Water Damage in Spartanburg

A bathroom is designed to handle water on its surfaces, never below them. When caulk cracks, grout wears thin or a toilet seal fails, a little water slips past with every shower and every flush. That water gathers where you cannot see it, in the subfloor and inside the tiled wall. Our Spartanburg team finds where the water is getting in, measures how far it has gone, and dries or removes what is wet.

Slow leaks are the norm here, which means what you find has typically been there longer, and gone further, than it seems. The tile can look perfectly fine while the subfloor beneath the toilet has gone soft clear through. Our crew maps the wet area using meters and a thermal camera so nothing extra gets torn out, and only spots that cannot dry where they are get opened.

Signs to Look For

  • A spongy feel in the floor beside the tub or in front of the vanity
  • Dark grout that never seems to dry out between showers
  • Musty odor in the bathroom that cleaning does not get rid of
  • Edges of the vinyl floor peeling up, or dark discoloration showing through it
  • The caulk line at the shower or tub has split, pulled away, or turned black
  • On the wall beside the shower, baseboard that has swelled or paint that is peeling
Our Process

How We Handle Bathroom Water Damage

1

Find the Leak

One by one we check the supply lines, toilet, shower and tub, then follow the water under the tile and into the walls with meters and a thermal camera.

2

Remove What Is Ruined

We take out rotted subfloor, soft wall board and tile that has come loose. If the toilet was the source, we handle that section as contaminated, and porous materials we cannot clean go.

3

Dry the Hidden Spaces

Air movers and dehumidifiers dry the subfloor, the wall framing and the ceiling space below. Before anything gets closed up, antimicrobial is applied and dry readings are confirmed.

4

Rebuild and Seal

After replacing backer board and subfloor, we can arrange tile work, a new seal at the toilet, fresh caulking and ceiling repair, which closes the leak path for real.

Behind the Surface

Risks That Are Easy to Miss

Damage Downstairs

Before dripping, water runs along joists and pipes, which can put the downstairs stain several feet away from the real leak. Wiring and light fixtures may lie along that route.

Leaks Inside the Vanity

Behind the towels and bottles kept under the sink, a drain fitting or supply valve can drip for months. The cabinet floor swells, and the water runs through to the subfloor and the wall behind it.

Poor Ventilation

A weak or missing exhaust fan leaves steam in the room after every shower. Mold feeds on the moisture that lands on ceilings and walls, and damp materials dry far more slowly.

A Failed Shower Pan

A cracked pan or liner lets water out under the shower floor every time it is used. Framing under the shower stays saturated even though the surface shows no flaw.

The Clock

The Damage Timeline

First days

Water Slips Through

Each shower pushes a little water through the failed caulk or grout. Nothing shows yet, but the subfloor edge and the board behind the tile are absorbing it.

First few weeks

Subfloor Stays Wet

Between showers, the wood below the tile has no time to dry out. Mold starts on the underside of the floor and inside the wall cavity.

One to three months

Signs Start to Appear

The caulk line goes black, the grout gets darker, and tiles work loose. A stain shows on the ceiling of any room below and slowly grows.

Six months and beyond

Structure Weakens

Subfloor around the toilet or tub turns soft and joists may begin to rot. The repair now includes carpentry and new tile, not only drying.
