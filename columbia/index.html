Simple Ways to Keep a Columbia Home Dry

Most of the water damage we see in Columbia homes started with something small that gave warning first. A few minutes of checking a couple of times a year catches most of it.

Start with supply lines. The rubber hoses on washing machines, dishwashers, toilets and ice makers wear out, and braided stainless replacements are inexpensive. Look under every sink for stains, swollen cabinet floors or a musty smell. At the water heater, watch for corrosion or moisture around the bottom, and flush some sediment out annually. Outside, keep gutters clear and make sure downspouts send water away from the foundation.

Find the main water shutoff now, and show every adult in the home how to close it. Small battery leak alarms near the washing machine, water heater and sinks give you an early warning for very little money. A stain that spreads or a floor that feels soft is worth a call, and Columbia homeowners are welcome to ask us about it.

Common Water Damage Issues in Columbia

Ask anyone on our crew what fills the schedule in Columbia and you will hear the same six answers. They are all common, and with every one of them waiting adds to the repair.

Mold: Mold is usually the second chapter of a water problem that someone thought was finished. Fans and towels dry what you can see, while the wall cavity, subfloor and carpet pad stay damp underneath. Since colonies can form in 24 to 48 hours, the first hint is often an earthy smell or speckled drywall weeks later.

Hoses: The braided or rubber lines behind toilets, sinks and washing machines are under pressure all day. A burst connector keeps spraying until somebody closes a valve, and that may take a long while in an empty house. The low-cost defense is to swap out aging hoses and learn where the main shutoff is.

Pumps: Sump pumps, where a home has one, tend to fail at the worst moment after years of running without attention. What we usually see is a jammed float, a motor that has worn out or a breaker that tripped. The result is several inches of water over the lowest floor, soaking stored boxes, flooring and the bottom of the walls. Testing the pump now and then, and adding a battery backup, lowers the odds considerably.

Toilets: Among bathroom emergencies in Columbia, the toilet that will not stop rising tops the list. It matters where the water came from, because tank or supply water is clean and used bowl water is not. In both cases it seeps beneath cabinets and flooring edges, and a moisture meter shows how far it went.

Cooling: Few people think about the small pipe that carries condensation away from an AC system. A rusted pan or a plugged line lets that water run into nearby flooring or the ceiling below. Because it happens slowly, the damage in many Columbia homes is well along before a stain appears. Most of these are avoided by getting the drain cleaned annually ahead of heavy use.

Flooding: When storm drains cannot keep up with a downpour, surface water heads for the lowest doorway it can find. It reaches ground-level rooms, garages and walkout lower levels on the downhill side before anything else. We pump it out, discard materials that cannot be sanitized and keep equipment running until moisture readings are back to normal.
